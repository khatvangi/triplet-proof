Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, "Triplet architecture enables deep error-minimization of the genetic code," for publication as an Original Article in *Journal of Molecular Evolution*.

The standard genetic code has been studied as an error-minimizing mapping for sixty years, but every Monte Carlo analysis in the literature has been confined to a single codon length: triplet. As a result, the optimization observed in the standard genetic code (SGC) has not been decomposable into the contribution of amino acid assignments versus the contribution of triplet architecture itself. We resolve this ambiguity with a 2 x 2 factorial design that crosses codon length (n = 2 vs. n = 3) with alphabet size (10 vs. 20 amino acids), scoring each cell against a degeneracy-preserving Monte Carlo null of one million random codes. Two graph-theoretic metrics — Dirichlet energy and rate-distortion noise distortion, both specializations of the codon-channel framework introduced by Tlusty (2007, 2010) and applied recently by Radvanyi and Kun (2021) — give concordant results.

The headline finding is a threshold effect. The standard code at n = 3 lies outside one million matched-degeneracy random codes on both metrics (z = -16.6 on noise distortion, z = -14.6 on Dirichlet energy; p < 10^-6). A 10-amino-acid triplet projection of the same code is similarly outside its matched null (z = -14.8). The 10-amino-acid doublet projection of the SGC, by contrast, reaches only z = -3.8, with the best random doublet within one z-unit. Triplet architecture therefore crosses an optimization threshold that doublet architecture cannot, independent of alphabet size. The mechanistic source is synonymy at the wobble position: position 3 of the triplet code absorbs 69-77% of single-nucleotide errors as silent substitutions, a geometric degree of freedom that doublet codes structurally cannot provide.

We believe this work suits *Journal of Molecular Evolution* for three reasons. First, it directly addresses a question framed by Woese (1965) and quantitatively pursued by Haig and Hurst (1991), Freeland and Hurst (1998), and Koonin and Novozhilov (2009). Second, it strengthens the Freeland-Hurst result by approximately two orders of magnitude (p < 10^-6 versus their ~10^-4), using PCA-derived properties that remove the property-selection degree of freedom identified by Koonin and Novozhilov (2009) as a persistent ambiguity in the optimization literature. Third, it specifies the architectural locus of the optimization that Novozhilov, Wolf, and Koonin (2007) characterized as "partial," clarifying what is partial about it: the alphabet assignment, not the codon length.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission to *Journal of Molecular Evolution*. The authors declare no conflict of interest.
